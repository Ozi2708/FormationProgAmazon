3.1
3.1 — Cookies and identity

Third-party cookies haven’t gone away, but they are no longer enough

Two forces are at work in parallel: browsers on one side, regulation on the other.

Safari
Third-party cookie blocked

Blocked by default. In this environment, the third-party cookie is already gone.

Firefox
Third-party cookie blocked

Also blocked by default, through Enhanced Tracking Protection.

Chrome
Kept — user choice

In April 2025, Google dropped its plan to remove third-party cookies and now leaves the choice to users.

On the regulatory side

GDPR and the ePrivacy Directive require consent. Without consent, no cookie can be set or read. A significant share of users refuse, which means just as much audience that cookies can’t address.

Four operational consequences

  • Cookie-based addressable reach varies widely from one browser to another.
  • Retargeting and cross-site frequency capping become less reliable on the open web.
  • Multi-touch measurement degrades: statistical models take over.
  • Authenticated environments and first-party data gain value.
3.2 — The alternatives

Four families of solutions

These approaches complement each other: none replaces the cookie on its own, and most plans combine two or three of them.

01
Semantic and contextual

Fine-grained analysis of the context: word associations, images, the angle of the content — not just keyword matching. No identifier required.

Ease of activation
02
Unique ID and SSO

First-party identification through login: hashed email, shared industry IDs, user accounts. Effectiveness depends on the logged-in rate.

Ease of activation
03
Server-side and edge

Storage and syncing on the server, or on the device itself: the identifier becomes first-party data again. Requires technical work on the publisher side.

Ease of activation
04
Deals and publisher data

Private Auction, Preferred Deal or Programmatic Guaranteed built on the publisher’s segments, activated at source on its own inventory. It is the easiest solution to activate.

Ease of activation
3.3 — Amazon’s position

First-party signals in a logged-in environment

The foundation is logged-in inventory. On Amazon.fr, Prime Video, Twitch, Fire TV and Amazon Music, the audience is authenticated rather than inferred from a third-party cookie: recognition and frequency capping work from one screen to the next.

Shopping

What is bought, considered, bought again.

Browsing

Searches, categories viewed, comparisons.

Streaming

Content watched and listened to, while logged in.

Device

Screens used, from mobile to connected TV.

Four activation building blocks

01

Amazon Audiences

Segments built on shopping, browsing and streaming signals, exclusive to Amazon DSP.

02

Advertiser audiences

Hashed CRM, remarketing through the Amazon Ad Tag, lookalike audiences built on these bases.

03

Amazon Marketing Cloud

Clean room for cross-dataset analysis and custom audiences, without exposing individual-level data.

04

Amazon Publisher Cloud

Publishers enrich their own inventory with Amazon signals before it goes to auction.

June 2026 — Amazon Publisher Cloud extends its Outcome Optimizer to programmatic guaranteed streaming deals.

3.5 — CTV and addressable TV

The number one growth driver in video inventory

Reference metrics: CPM, reach, completion. CTV is planned on incremental reach, not on clicks.

CTV

Full-screen video served on connected TV, within streaming platforms and CTV apps: non-skippable spots, a premium and brand-safe context.

At Amazon: Prime Video, Twitch, Fire TV Channels and third-party apps.

Addressable TV

Linear TV addressed by household: the standard TV spot is replaced by a message targeted on household data, through operator set-top boxes.

Constraint: a TV copy and an ARPP clearance (the French ad standards body) are still required.

The message is triggered by conditions

That is what programmatic changes in outdoor advertising. Pick a trigger: the creative served on the screen changes accordingly.

Key takeaways — chapter 3
Addressability is changing its foundation, not its nature.
  • Third-party cookies survive in Chrome, but their coverage is partial and uneven.
  • Context, first-party IDs, server-side and publisher data work together: none replaces everything.
  • Audio, CTV and DOOH are going programmatic and are bought from the same entry point.
  • Authenticated environments and first-party data mechanically gain value.
